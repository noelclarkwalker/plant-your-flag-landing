# SITE_INTERACTION_LANGUAGE.md

# NoelClark.com Version 1.0
## Governing Creative Authority

---

# Purpose

This document defines the universal interaction language of NoelClark.com.

It governs how the website behaves, responds, reveals, and communicates through movement.

It is not an engineering document.

It does not prescribe implementation technologies, animation libraries, or technical solutions.

Instead, it defines the creative philosophy that every interaction throughout NoelClark.com must express.

Where page-specific interaction authorities exist (Portal, Homepage, Features, future experiences), they inherit from this document rather than replacing it.

---

# The Website Is Alive

NoelClark.com should never feel static.

Likewise, it should never feel busy.

The world feels alive because it behaves naturally.

Movement should feel inevitable rather than performed.

The visitor should never become aware of animation as a feature.

Instead, they should simply experience a world that quietly responds to curiosity.

---

# Interaction Exists To Reveal

Interaction is never decoration.

Interaction always reveals meaning.

Visitors should discover understanding through participation rather than receive explanation immediately.

Whenever possible:

Curiosity precedes explanation.

Exploration precedes understanding.

Discovery precedes invitation.

---

# Motion Has Purpose

Movement must always communicate something meaningful.

Motion should never exist merely because animation is available.

Every movement should answer one of the following questions:

- What is being revealed?
- What is changing?
- What is becoming understood?
- What is inviting the visitor forward?

If movement cannot answer one of those questions, it likely does not belong.

---

# Motion Is Concentrated, Not Continuous

Motion should be intentionally scarce.

At any given moment, only the current interaction and the elements essential to communicating that interaction should animate.

Everything else should remain still.

This creates clarity.

This protects performance.

Most importantly, it gives every movement narrative significance.

The world should never compete with itself for the visitor's attention.

---

# Every Interaction Has A Reason

Objects behave according to their nature.

Letters unfold.

Magnifying glasses investigate.

Records rotate.

Ink writes.

Plants sway.

Paper moves.

Clouds drift.

Nothing moves simply because movement is possible.

Objects should always behave in ways that feel authentic to what they are.

---

# Discovery Over Presentation

Visitors should rarely be presented with information immediately.

Instead, NoelClark.com rewards curiosity.

Information is uncovered.

Messages are revealed.

Ideas emerge naturally through interaction.

The visitor should consistently feel:

"I found this."

rather than:

"The website showed me this."

---

# The Living Environment

Environmental movement exists to create life, not distraction.

Examples include:

- plants gently responding to air
- paper reacting to handling
- reflections changing with perspective
- clouds slowly drifting
- water quietly moving
- hanging objects responding to gravity
- light subtly changing across a surface

Environmental movement should never become the primary focus of a scene.

It supports atmosphere.

It does not compete with story.

---

# Motion Vocabulary

Throughout NoelClark.com, movement should communicate through a consistent visual language.

Preferred motion verbs include:

- Reveal
- Unfold
- Discover
- Investigate
- Breathe
- Drift
- Write
- Listen
- Illuminate
- Invite
- Progress
- Gather
- Press
- Collect

These verbs describe the emotional purpose of motion rather than its technical implementation.

---

# Motion That Does Not Belong

The following types of movement conflict with the creative authority of NoelClark.com and should generally be avoided unless explicitly approved elsewhere:

- movement created only to impress
- decorative animation without narrative purpose
- competing simultaneous focal animations
- excessive spectacle
- aggressive attention-seeking transitions
- motion that interrupts reading or reflection
- movement that feels artificial or mechanical without narrative justification

Visitors should feel guided, never overwhelmed.

---

# Narrative Rhythm

Meaningful interactions throughout NoelClark.com generally follow the same emotional rhythm:

Arrival

↓

Curiosity

↓

Discovery

↓

Understanding

↓

Invitation

↓

Progression

Not every interaction requires every stage.

However, this rhythm should inform the overall experience of the website.

---

# Performance Is Part Of The Experience

Performance is not merely an engineering concern.

Performance is part of the creative experience.

Interactions should remain responsive and effortless across supported devices.

Whenever two creative approaches communicate the same idea equally well, preference should be given to the approach that is simpler, more resilient, and more performant.

The visitor should never become aware of technical complexity.

Only the experience should remain visible.

---

# Consistency

Every new interaction added to NoelClark.com should feel like it belongs to the same world.

Visitors may not consciously notice this consistency.

They should simply feel that every interaction speaks the same visual language.

The interaction language of NoelClark.com is therefore not defined by specific animations.

It is defined by purpose, restraint, curiosity, and discovery.

Every movement should deepen the visitor's relationship with the world rather than distract from it.

---

# Governing Principle

NoelClark.com is not experienced through animation.

Animation is one of the ways NoelClark.com communicates.

The goal is never to impress visitors with movement.

The goal is to help visitors feel that curiosity itself has become a place they can explore.

When visitors remember NoelClark.com, they should remember what they discovered, not the animations that revealed it.
